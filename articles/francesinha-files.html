11 — so what does

The thing that wins games

We tested every statistic we had against career win rate. Four of them, ranked by how much of your winning they explain. The answer is not in the scoring column.

Kiko TM scores 0.9 a game and wins 60% of them. Zé SF scores 2.1 and wins 47%. That is the whole finding in two men.

One honest caveat. Goals conceded, on its own, already says a lot — it correlates with winning at r = −0.69. It just says less than goals prevented does, and here is why: a high-scoring game is high-scoring at both ends (r = +0.28 between a team's goals for and against), so conceding few is partly just landing in quiet games. What separates players is not how many go in behind them, but how many fewer go in because they are there — which is exactly what the top bar measures.

12 — the audit

The record that never happened

The database says Bernardo Xavier scored twenty-two goals on 7 April 2022. His team won that game 11–10. You cannot score twenty-two goals in an eleven-goal team.

Struck from the record

Bernardo Xavier, 22 goals, 7 April 2022

Impossible. Along with a second entry — nine goals on 6 March 2025, in a game his side scored five. These are the only two rows in all 2,037 appearances where a player out-scores his own team. Together they inflate his career total by 36%: from 85 goals to 116.

The actual record

Zi, 9 goals, 15 July 2021

A 20–14 win, and the highest-scoring game in league history at 34 goals. That is the real one.

Unresolved

926 goals have no owner

The scorelines add up to 3,216 goals. The names attached to them add up to 2,321. Twenty-nine percent of every goal ever scored in this league belongs to nobody — consistently, in every single edition. Own goals, forgotten scorers, or a long tradition of nobody writing it down.

Two findings were cut outright rather than corrected. "Fanuca owns Mini Zi" dissolved once we accounted for Mini Zi being beatable by absolutely everyone. And a claim that a three-time champion had finished stone last turned out to be a miscount — twelfth of fourteen, with Zi propping up the table.

Method

Every figure here comes from the league's own database — 204 MasterLeague games between 15 October 2020 and 27 August 2026 — and every one was re-run independently before it went on this page. Duo claims need at least fifteen games together, trios at least ten, and per-player rates at least thirty appearances. Where a stat is a fun coincidence rather than a real effect, it says so.

Updated on 2 September 2026 with the game of 27 August, which was missing from the database. One game in 204 was enough to change who won the sixth edition.

Several findings that looked excellent did not survive that process and are not here. The three most tempting are named in file 12, on the grounds that a record book you cannot argue with is no fun at all.
